The world, two ways — no global pie*

There is no single honest global pie. Measured in liters, Monster is bigger; measured in dollars, Red Bull is bigger — because Red Bull charges about twice as much per liter. So instead of one chart, flip between the two ways of counting.

By dollars — value, 2024 estimate*

Estimated share of global retail dollars. These are ranges, not a published figure — the bar is drawn at the middle of each range.

Red Bull 25–30%
Monster 15–20%
All others ~50–60%
Red Bull ~25–30% Monster ~15–20% All others ~50–60%

This is our estimate, not a published Euromonitor figure. Method, short version: Red Bull charges about $3.18 per liter at manufacturer level vs Monster's about $1.56 — roughly 2x. So dividing manufacturer revenue by the ~$80B retail market understates retail share by about 2x. The 25–30% range applies a US-observed retail/manufacturer markup (~1.8x) instead.

By units — liters, 2023–24*

Share of the actual liquid sold worldwide. This flips the ranking from the dollar view: Monster sells more of it, Red Bull charges more for it. The bar is drawn at the middle of each range.

Red Bull ~13%
Monster 16–17%
All others ~70%
Red Bull ~13% Monster ~16–17% All others ~70%

On volume, the best-available shares are roughly Red Bull ~13% and Monster ~16–17% of global liters (2023–24, secondary summaries). Euromonitor Passport — the source behind most global share figures — is paywalled, so treat these as approximate.

Sources: Monster ~4.8B liters from 846.7M 192-oz cases, 2024, Monster Beverage 10-K. Red Bull ~3.5–4.0B liters from 12.67B cans sold, 2024, company-reported — liters estimated from the can mix, so a range is shown.

*Approximate — these global figures are estimates, not precise published shares. Unlike the US pie above, which comes from one Nielsen table, no single published source splits the whole world this way: the dollar ranges are our estimate (method shown in the dollar view) and the liter shares are best-available approximations from secondary summaries. Read them as “about this big,” not as exact numbers.

Where 43% came from: You will see “Red Bull 43% of the global market” everywhere. That was Red Bull's share of world value in 2011 (Euromonitor). The same source had it at 21.5% of world volume, and Monster at 16% — not 39%. The 43/39 pair is a US list from around 2015–2020 that got relabeled “global”.

KDP's 10.1% is Q3 2026 (KDP earnings call, Nov 3, 2026), a later quarter, and is not in this pie. Globally there is no pie: volume shares are roughly Red Bull ~13% and Monster ~16–17% of liters (approximate), and the dollar ranges (Red Bull ~25–30%, Monster ~15–20%) are this site's estimate with the method shown above, not a published Euromonitor figure.

Ownership notes: the panels above keep it simple — they list the brands each company distributes. Who actually owns them is messier. Ghost Energy is 60% owned by Keurig Dr Pepper. Venom Energy is 100% owned by Keurig Dr Pepper. C4 Energy is owned by Nutrabolt — Keurig Dr Pepper owns about 36% of Nutrabolt and distributes C4. Bloom Sparkling Energy is owned by Bloom Nutrition; Keurig Dr Pepper distributes it, and its stake in Bloom is indirect, through Nutrabolt. Black Rifle Energy is owned by Black Rifle Coffee Company, and Keurig Dr Pepper distributes it. Rockstar is owned by Celsius Holdings in the US and Canada only — PepsiCo owns Rockstar everywhere else.
